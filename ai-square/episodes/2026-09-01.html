Full transcript

Maya: Agentic AI is making its way into banks, factory floors, and open source frameworks at full speed. But are we actually keeping up on the security side? Welcome to AI Square, the podcast that breaks down artificial intelligence in your everyday world. It is Tuesday, September 1st, 2026, and we have a packed show today. Trust me, some of what we are covering is going to make you think twice before you spin up your next AI agent.

Maya: Let's kick things off with the biggest story of the day, and this one hits close to home for developers building AI agents with the Elixir framework. Ryan, six critical vulnerabilities found in ashai — how serious are we talking?

Ryan: Very serious, Maya. Quick bit of context: ashai is a widely used framework in the Elixir ecosystem, specifically built for creating AI agents. Today we got a coordinated disclosure of six CVEs — six officially registered security flaws — and they cover the entire agent stack. We are talking remote code execution, authentication bypass, credential leaks, infinite loops that can take down a whole system, and even DNS rebinding, which is a pretty sophisticated attack technique.

Maya: DNS rebinding — for our listeners who are not deep in the developer world, what does that actually mean in plain English?

Ryan: So the simple version is this: it is a technique that lets an outside attacker trick an AI agent into thinking it is talking to a trusted service, when it is actually communicating with a malicious server. Picture an AI agent handling sensitive company data — with this vulnerability, someone could feed it fraudulent instructions and walk away with confidential information. What is really notable here is that this is the first major coordinated disclosure for an Elixir-specific AI framework. It is a sign that the security research community is starting to take the AI agent ecosystem seriously.

Maya: And is there any good news in all of this?

Ryan: There is. All six vulnerabilities are patched in version 1.0.0 of the framework. So if you are using ashai, the message is straightforward: update right now, no delay. But beyond the patch itself, this story raises a fundamental question. AI agents, unlike traditional applications, have autonomous action capabilities. They can call APIs, execute code, interact with databases. A vulnerability in that context can have consequences that are orders of magnitude more serious than in a conventional app.

Maya: It is basically what you could call the risk multiplier effect of agentic AI.

Ryan: Exactly. And that theme is going to come right back up when we talk about the Broadcom announcements coming up next.

Maya: Let's get into it, because September 1st, 2026 is a genuinely busy day on the tools and frameworks front. Ryan, we have several major announcements. Where do you want to start?

Ryan: Let's start with Broadcom, which is dropping two distinct but complementary things today. First up is VMware Private AI Cloud — a full platform for building, running, and governing AI agents in a completely private environment, meaning no reliance on the public cloud. It supports more than 150 open source models and provides an end-to-end framework for moving from pilot phases all the way to real production agent deployments.

Maya: And the second launch from Broadcom is AgentMinder?

Ryan: That's right. AgentMinder is essentially Broadcom's direct answer to the kind of security gaps we were just talking about. They are positioning it as a security solution built specifically for enterprise AI agents. It covers tool abuse prevention — so stopping an agent from accessing functions it has no business touching — zero-day attack detection, and what they call Zero Trust microsegmentation at the hypervisor level. For folks less familiar with that term: the hypervisor is the lowest layer of a virtualized infrastructure. Securing AI agents at that level is an extremely robust approach.

Maya: Is Broadcom alone in going after this agentic AI security space?

Ryan: Not at all, and that is what makes it interesting. Where Broadcom does stand out, though, is that it is one of the first legacy infrastructure players — think VMware, think their whole history in virtualization — to natively integrate AI agent security into its core offering. That is a strong signal that agentic AI is no longer just a startup conversation. It is moving into the deep layers of enterprise infrastructure.

Maya: We also have a major update on the OpenClaw side of things. What exactly is that project?

Ryan: OpenClaw is a popular open source framework for building personal AI agents. Version 2.0 brings some significant improvements: better memory persistence for agents, a simplified installation process that makes it more accessible to less experienced developers, improved interaction quality, and additional security measures. What stands out is what this project represents — democratized access to AI agents. You do not need enterprise infrastructure. Anyone can deploy their own agents locally. Version 2.0 is a real marker of growing maturity for this tool.

Maya: Now on the research side, we have an announcement coming out of the robotics world, and honestly Ryan, when I read this one this morning I thought it was pretty remarkable.

Ryan: Skild AI is unveiling S1, and yeah, remarkable is the right word. S1 is a foundation model built specifically for robotics. What makes it stand out is its ability to do in-context learning from a single video. Practically speaking, you show a robot a video of a complex task — assembling a part, sorting objects — and the robot learns to replicate that task. One visual demonstration is all it takes.

Maya: Up until now, robots needed thousands of examples to learn a task, right?

Ryan: That is exactly the paradigm shift here. Traditional robotics approaches require hundreds or even thousands of demonstrations, very long training phases, and task-specific specialization. S1 takes what we have learned from large language models — where in-context learning allows generalization from very few examples — and applies that to the physical world. The practical implication is huge: getting a robot up and running on a new task could go from taking several weeks down to just a few hours.

Maya: And this also ties into the TUSS technology from Mitsubishi Electric that we heard about today?

Ryan: The connection is indirect but it fits together well. Mitsubishi Electric is presenting TUSS — which stands for Task-Aware Unified Source Separation — an AI model capable of separating complex sounds in difficult acoustic environments, like a loud factory floor or a crowded public space. Think about a robot or an AI system that needs to understand spoken instructions in an environment full of machinery noise, overlapping conversations, background music — TUSS lets it zero in on what actually matters. It is a foundational building block for making physical AI systems reliable in the real world, not just in quiet lab conditions.

Maya: So S1 to see and learn, TUSS to hear through the noise — the building blocks of physical AI are really coming together.

Ryan: Well said. And when those capabilities are combined with secure frameworks like what Broadcom is putting forward, you start to have the real foundations for AI agents that can operate reliably in industrial environments.

Maya: Let's talk industry impact now, because agentic AI is not staying in the lab — it is walking right into the banking sector. Ryan, Kyndryl and Google Cloud — what exactly did they deploy?

Ryan: This is a concrete and very significant case. Kyndryl — the major IT infrastructure services company that was spun off from IBM — has partnered with Google Cloud to deploy AI agents using Gemini models at two financial institutions: Incore Bank and Suryoday Small Finance Bank. The use cases are specific and genuinely critical: customer onboarding and risk assessment.

Maya: Bank onboarding — that is the process of bringing a new customer into the system?

Ryan: Exactly — identity verification, document collection, risk profile analysis, account opening. In a traditional bank, that process can drag on for days, with a lot of human touchpoints and real risk of error. The AI agents deployed here fully automate that entire flow. For Incore Bank, the proof of concept has been validated successfully. What is striking is how the partnership is structured: Kyndryl brings deep expertise in complex banking information systems, Google Cloud brings Gemini. It is a genuinely complementary alliance.

Maya: On the markets side, Trade Desk is up five percent following the launch of Kokai Zuma. What does that tell you about investor appetite for agentic AI?

Ryan: It says a lot. Trade Desk, for those who may not know, is a programmatic advertising platform where professional traders buy ad inventory in real time. Kokai Zuma is their agentic AI platform, which improves conversion tracking, reporting, and trader workflows. A five percent single-day jump for a major publicly traded company is significant. It reflects genuine market conviction that agentic AI is not just a trend — it is a measurable competitive advantage. And what is worth noting is that competitors like AppLovin and Magnite barely moved in the same window. The market is clearly drawing a line between the companies that are actually executing and the ones that are still just talking about it.

Maya: We also want to flag a practical resource for our listeners — a roundup of the 20 best memory and context management tools for AI agents in production in 2026.

Ryan: This is a landscape overview published by StartupHub.ai, and the timing is perfect, because memory management is one of the most concrete challenges when you are actually deploying agents in production. An AI agent often needs to maintain context across long interactions, remember user preferences, manage complex states — and the tools for doing that well have multiplied fast. This guide is a solid starting point for technical teams who are moving into real-world agent deployment.

Maya: To wrap up today's episode, Ryan, if you had to identify the single thread running through this whole September 1st, 2026 news cycle, what would it be?

Ryan: The thread is the tension between accelerated deployment and the security that needs to keep pace with it. We are seeing AI agents move into critical systems — banks, factory floors, enterprise infrastructure. At the same time, the vulnerabilities revealed in ashai are a reminder that the agentic AI attack surface is new and still not fully understood. And the responses are coming: Broadcom with AgentMinder and VMware Private AI Cloud, the ashai team shipping fixes fast. Maturity gets built exactly this way — through cycles of deployment, discovery of vulnerabilities, and reinforcement. There is something reassuring about that, even if each flaw can cause real damage in the meantime.

Maya: An ecosystem that moves fast and learns from its mistakes — that might just be the best way to describe agentic AI in 2026. Ryan, thanks so much for breaking all of this down, and thank you to everyone who stuck with us through the whole episode.
