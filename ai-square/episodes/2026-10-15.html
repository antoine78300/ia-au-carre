Full transcript

Maya: Welcome to AI Squared, your daily dose of everything you need to know about artificial intelligence. It is Thursday, October 15th, 2026, and agentic AI is everywhere right now: inside your bank, running your ad campaigns, moving things around in warehouses, and even watching over the planet from space. Today we are breaking down all of it, and trust me, there is a lot to get through.

Maya: Ryan, let's kick things off with some big industry recognition that is turning heads in the enterprise world. IBM just got named a leader in agentic AI platforms by the IDC MarketScape. What exactly is this ranking, and what does it tell us about where the market stands right now?

Ryan: Great place to start, Maya. The IDC MarketScape is one of the most closely watched evaluations among IT decision-makers at major companies around the world. Landing a leader designation there is not something you stumble into. It means IBM convinced the analysts on two fronts: the strength of their strategic vision, and their ability to actually execute on it today, not just on paper.

Maya: And what does that look like in practice? What are they actually offering?

Ryan: IBM is pushing their watsonx Orchestrate platform front and center. The core idea is giving enterprises the ability to manage, govern, and secure their AI agents across the entire lifecycle. And the piece that really matters here is what they call runtime independence. Basically, it does not matter where your AI agent is actually running, whether that is on-premises, in the cloud, or in a hybrid setup. watsonx Orchestrate keeps you in control of it regardless.

Maya: So IBM is essentially saying: no matter how messy and complicated your infrastructure is, we give you one single dashboard to run all your agents from.

Ryan: Exactly. And that message is really hitting home right now because the real headache for large enterprises is no longer building AI agents. It is keeping tabs on them once they start multiplying across the organization. We are talking governance, security, auditability. Those are the real challenges in agentic AI in 2026.

Maya: And that whole question of governance actually connects directly to another announcement today, this one from Netzilo. Because the problem they are tackling is even more complex.

Ryan: It is, and honestly this was one of the announcements that caught my attention most today. Netzilo AI Edge is launching something they call Drift Control, and the concept is really worth unpacking. Picture an AI agent that has been set up to do one specific job, let's say negotiating supply purchases within a set budget. Now picture that same agent running simultaneously across several completely different environments: a local endpoint, a web browser, a cloud-based SaaS app, a serverless function.

Maya: And the risk is that the agent starts going off-script in one of those environments without anyone noticing?

Ryan: That is exactly what Netzilo calls drift. And their answer to it is a unified governance layer that covers all of those runtimes at once. To actually catch the drift when it happens, they are using two complementary mechanisms: their System One models on one side, and LLM judges on the other, meaning language models that are constantly evaluating agent behavior and checking that it stays within its defined scope.

Maya: It is almost like having an air traffic controller assigned to each AI agent.

Ryan: I love that analogy. And that is really how Netzilo is positioning this, as the solution to what they call the critical bottleneck of distributed governance. Up until now, each runtime had its own oversight mechanisms, or sometimes none at all. Getting one unified view across all of them is a meaningful step forward.

Maya: Alright, let's move into our tools and frameworks segment, and we have got two big announcements here. First Postman with their Fabric Gateway, and then OnAudience using the MCP protocol. Ryan, let's start with Postman because this is a significant architectural move.

Ryan: Postman is a name every developer knows. It is the go-to tool for testing and documenting APIs. And now they are making a bold strategic bet with the launch of Fabric Gateway. The idea is to put a centralized control layer between AI agents and the entire ecosystem they interact with: over a thousand large language model providers, MCP servers, and APIs of all kinds.

Maya: So Postman is positioning itself as the mandatory checkpoint, kind of like a central hub for all agentic traffic.

Ryan: That is exactly right. And what is new and confirmed with this announcement is that a single gateway can now route to the entire LLM and MCP ecosystem in production. This is not theoretical anymore, it is live. And analysts are starting to identify a three-layer architectural pattern emerging: your agents on one side, this gateway in the middle acting as a control plane, and all your providers and services on the other end. A de facto standard is starting to take shape here.

Maya: And that connects naturally to the OnAudience announcement, which is using MCP in the advertising space?

Ryan: Right, and this is a solid real-world example of MCP being put to use in a specific industry. OnAudience is launching an MCP server that connects AI assistants directly to their audience data. They have more than four thousand one hundred pre-built audience segments. The idea is that a media planner can now just describe in plain language what they are looking for. For example, say you want to target urban women between twenty-five and thirty-five who are into sports and wellness with a campaign budget of one hundred thousand dollars, and the agent automatically builds the matching audience profile without anyone having to touch a technical interface.

Maya: So are we looking at the end of Excel spreadsheets in media planning?

Ryan: Let's say it is the beginning of a pretty deep transformation of the profession. What is really notable here is that the MCP protocol, which is a standardized way for AI agents to connect to external data sources, is starting to take hold in very concrete sectors like advertising. This is no longer a developers-only conversation.

Maya: Perfect. Let's take a quick breather and come back with some pure research, and this one is coming from Google. AI agents analyzing our planet from space. That really puts things in perspective, Ryan.

Ryan: This was honestly one of the announcements that hit me hardest today. Google Research is deploying geospatial reasoning agents, meaning agents that can analyze satellite and geographic data to tackle complex problems at a truly planetary scale.

Maya: When we say complex problems at a planetary scale, what does that actually mean on the ground?

Ryan: Think about tracking deforestation in real time, assessing the damage from a natural disaster, monitoring how coastlines are changing as sea levels rise, or optimizing agricultural irrigation networks across an entire continent. These are spatial analysis challenges that general-purpose language models simply cannot handle because they were never designed to reason in geographic space.

Maya: So what is Google doing differently?

Ryan: Their approach combines three elements: foundation models that are purpose-built for geospatial data, tools that know how to work with spatial data like map projections and geographic correlation analysis, and a planning mechanism that lets the agent chain together multiple steps of spatial reasoning. And to back all of this up, they are releasing two new benchmarks: GeoKTools and GeoExpertQueries, which will let the research community actually measure how models perform on these geospatial tasks.

Maya: Is this accessible beyond Google's own research labs?

Ryan: That is exactly what makes this announcement land. Google is opening access through two channels: Ask Google Earth for end users, and an Agent-to-Agent service for developers who want to plug these capabilities into their own systems. It is the leap from the lab to the real world.

Maya: And speaking of access to research, Google made another announcement today in Dublin, Ryan, and it touches on the geopolitics of AI.

Ryan: Yes, and this dimension is often underestimated. Google is announcing a program that gives two hundred European researchers direct access to their most advanced AI models and serious compute resources, unveiled at an AI summit organized in Dublin. The stated goal is to accelerate European innovation in AI research, but there is clearly also a strategic play here in terms of positioning amid the competition between major tech powers.

Maya: It is also a way for Google to build relationships with the European academic ecosystem.

Ryan: Exactly. And for European researchers who do not always have access to the compute infrastructure needed to work on the most advanced models, this is a real, tangible opportunity. The question that will need to be answered down the line is what the conditions of that access look like, and who owns the intellectual output of the research that comes out of it.

Maya: Let's move into our industry impact segment now, and we have got three announcements that show agentic AI planting roots in very concrete sectors. Let's start with robotics, Ryan. Neura Robotics is announcing something pretty striking.

Ryan: Neura Robotics is a company building humanoid robots, and their announcement today centers on Neuron AI, their multimodal foundation model. What sets it apart is the integration of multiple sensory modalities: three-dimensional vision, touch, audio, the robot's internal states, and language. All of that in a single model.

Maya: And what exactly is persistent memory in this context?

Ryan: That is probably the most remarkable capability here. One of the classic problems in robotics is that when an object moves out of the robot's field of view, the robot loses track of it mentally. With Neuron AI, the robot holds onto context even when objects disappear from its visual field. It knows the object still exists, where it was, and can keep planning its actions accordingly.

Maya: That is basically a working memory for robots.

Ryan: Well put. And this model was trained on real robot data collected in what Neura calls Neura Gyms, dedicated physical training environments. This is not simulation data. It is the real world. And that fundamentally changes how robust the model is.

Maya: Let's shift over to finance now. Bank of America is bringing agentic automation into its CashPro treasury management platform. That is a major signal.

Ryan: A very major one, yes. CashPro is Bank of America's cash management platform used by institutional clients, large corporations, and funds. The fact that Tom Durkin, the head of CashPro, is publicly walking through BofA's AI investments and the move toward autonomous agents for liquidity management is about as strong a validation as you can get.

Maya: And how does it actually work in practice?

Ryan: What is interesting about this deployment is the explicit balance being maintained between autonomous automation and human oversight. This is not a replacement for treasury teams. It is an upgrade to their capabilities. The agents handle multi-step analyses, liquidity placement recommendations, and position monitoring, while the human teams stay in control of the critical decisions. That hybrid model is really becoming the standard in financial services.

Maya: And we will wrap up with TalentNeuron, which is pushing AI agents into HR through their Synapse platform. Used by sixty percent of the Fortune 100. That is a massive footprint.

Ryan: That is a genuinely significant adoption signal, yes. TalentNeuron is adding agentic capabilities to Synapse, their AI assistant built for workforce analytics. The agents can now plan and execute multi-step analyses without any human in the loop: analyzing how skills are shifting in a given market, spotting where talent is getting scarce for certain profiles, and automatically generating presentation-ready reports for executive committees.

Maya: So an HR leader can ask Synapse to put together a full analysis of the AI engineering market in Europe ahead of the next board meeting, and the agent just handles the whole thing end to end?

Ryan: That is exactly the use case. And what validates the real value of this announcement is that Synapse is drawing on proprietary labor market data, not generic data. The analyses are sourced and verifiable. That is the difference between an agent that hallucinates trends and an agent that is actually working from real numbers.

Maya: And that brings us to the end of a really packed edition. Ryan, if you had to pull one thread that runs through everything we covered today, what would it be?

Ryan: The through line is pretty clear to me: the operational maturity of agentic AI. We are not talking about experiments or demos anymore. IBM is being recognized as a global leader in a market that is actively structuring itself. Netzilo is solving real governance problems in production environments. Bank of America is running agents inside its institutional treasury operations. And Neura is building robots with persistent memory trained on real-world data. Agentic AI is no longer a promise. It is infrastructure that is being rolled out at scale.

Maya: And the governance and control questions we kept coming back to throughout the show, with Netzilo, with Postman, with IBM, those show that the ecosystem is starting to take the risks of these large-scale deployments seriously.

Ryan: That is the sign of a market that is growing up. Governance is not glamorous, but it is what lets you go further with confidence.

Maya: Ryan, thank you as always for breaking it all down. And thank you to everyone listening for sticking with us all the way through. If this episode was useful to you, share it with someone who would appreciate it, and make sure you are subscribed to AI Squared on whatever platform you are listening on. We will see you tomorrow for another edition. See you then!
