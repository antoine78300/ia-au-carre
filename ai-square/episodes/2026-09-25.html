Maya: Good morning and welcome to AI Squared, the podcast that breaks down artificial intelligence for you every single day. Today is Friday, September 25th, 2026, and we have a packed show lined up: an AI making a breakthrough biology discovery, major banks going all-in on agentic AI, and some security alerts that deserve your full attention. Get comfortable, because we are diving right in.

Ryan: Good morning, Maya.

Maya: Good morning, Ryan. So let's kick things off with the biggest story of the day, because honestly, when I read this one this morning, I had to do a double take. Claude, the model from Anthropic, apparently discovered a brand-new biological system similar to CRISPR. Is that right?

Ryan: That is exactly right. And I totally get why that catches people off guard, because this is not a documentation task, it is not summarizing research papers. Claude worked autonomously inside a life sciences lab that Anthropic just launched, and in twenty-one hours, it identified a previously unknown enzymatic system inside viral DNA, specifically in bacteriophages. This system could represent an entirely new gene-editing mechanism, very much in the same vein as CRISPR.

Maya: Okay, let's back up for a second, because not everyone listening has a biology degree. Can we quickly remind people what CRISPR actually is?

Ryan: Of course. Think of CRISPR as molecular scissors. It lets scientists edit DNA with incredible precision. It has been a game changer in medicine, agriculture, and fundamental research. Over the past decade or so, it has completely transformed the life sciences. So when we talk about a potentially unknown system that works along similar lines, that is a headline-level announcement.

Maya: And this was found by an AI, on its own, in less than a day.

Ryan: Autonomously, yes. And what this really validates is that AI agents are no longer limited to admin tasks or generating text. We are talking about a complex scientific discovery, in a field where a human research team would need weeks, maybe months, to get to the same place. This is the first operational announcement from Anthropic that genuinely proves out this kind of cutting-edge research application.

Maya: Has the discovery been independently verified yet?

Ryan: Anthropic is presenting it as validated within their lab environment. The specifics around external peer validation are still forthcoming, but the announcement is official and has been picked up by credible outlets. We will obviously be watching for independent confirmation over the coming weeks. But what matters right now is what this tells us about where we are: AI agents are stepping into the arena of scientific discovery in a real and meaningful way.

Maya: Absolutely fascinating. Let's move on to the tools and frameworks space, because there are a few announcements worth digging into. Ryan, where do you want to start?

Ryan: I want to highlight two announcements that together paint a very clear picture of where things are headed: agentic AI is moving closer to the ground level. The first one is Google with its Antigravity SDK. Google is announcing that this developer kit now supports local AI models, specifically Gemma 4 26B A4B, through a technology called LiteRT.

Maya: Help me out here, Ryan. Why does it matter to be able to run an agent locally, without going through the cloud?

Ryan: Great question. Up until now, most AI agents needed a live connection to remote servers to function. That creates two major problems. The first is privacy: if your data is being sent to an external server, you lose control of it. The second is what we call air-gapped environments, meaning networks that are deliberately cut off from the internet, like you would find in certain military, industrial, or banking contexts. With this Google update, you can now deploy an AI agent directly on the device itself, with zero dependency on the cloud. That is a meaningful shift for governance and for agentic privacy.

Maya: And what is the second tools announcement?

Ryan: That one comes from Teradata with their system called Harness. The idea is to optimize how AI agents execute multi-step workflows. Concretely, before even calling a large language model, Harness builds an execution plan, applies eighty-four optimization patterns, batches independent tasks together, and cuts out any tool calls that are not contributing to the final result.

Maya: Eighty-four patterns, that is a very specific number. Does this translate to real cost savings?

Ryan: The stated goal is to reduce token costs, meaning the processing units that models charge you for, and to eliminate unproductive loops. But Teradata is upfront about the risk: if the system removes a step it judges as unnecessary and that step turns out to actually matter, the quality of the output can degrade. They are very clear that rigorous production-level testing is a must. It is a powerful tool, but it does not come without trade-offs.

Maya: We talk about agentic AI conceptually a lot, but this is getting into very real engineering challenges.

Ryan: Absolutely. And that is a sign the industry is maturing. We are not talking about demos anymore. We are talking about optimization, cost management, and production reliability. This is a professionalization phase.

Maya: Great. Let's shift to the research side, and specifically a topic that keeps coming back front and center: AI model security. Ryan, Cisco put out a major update to their security rankings.

Ryan: Yes, and this is a serious body of work. Cisco is updating what they call the LLM Security Leaderboard, a ranking that evaluates how robust models are against attacks. They have now tested a hundred and thirty-six models in total, with a hundred and two new entries since June. The big new addition this time around is coverage of multimodal models.

Maya: Multimodal meaning?

Ryan: Models that do not just process text, but also images and audio. Cisco added sixty-nine new multimodal entries, fifty-five on the image side and fourteen for audio. And there is a second major development: they are now incorporating metrics specifically designed for autonomous AI agents, testing multi-turn behaviors, meaning sequences of chained actions by an agent under real production conditions.

Maya: And how are the tests actually run?

Ryan: Without additional guardrails, meaning without the safety layers that are typically stacked on top of models in deployment. The idea is to produce a comparable baseline, essentially a raw test to see what the model actually does before any constraints are applied. It is methodologically sound and lets you make apples-to-apples comparisons across models.

Maya: That is really useful for companies trying to make an informed choice about which model to use.

Ryan: Exactly. And it takes on extra weight when you look at the other security story of the day, which is a lot more alarming.

Maya: Yes, let's talk about this vulnerability that was found in IBM's systems.

Ryan: This is a serious flaw. It affects IBM's Financial Transaction Manager, and it is a textbook example of what we call RAG poisoning. RAG, for listeners who have not heard that term, is a very common architecture where an AI agent pulls information from a document database to enrich its responses.

Maya: And what goes wrong?

Ryan: The problem is that if an attacker can inject malicious documents into that database, they can influence what the agent ends up doing. And in this case, without even needing to authenticate, a minimal injection of documents into the vector store is enough to hijack the agent's operations. The vulnerability maps to a recognized category in the OWASP framework, the international organization that catalogs AI system vulnerabilities, specifically LLM09 in their 2026 list, which covers weaknesses in vectors and embeddings.

Maya: So concretely, an agent managing financial transactions could be manipulated into executing unauthorized operations?

Ryan: That is exactly the risk. And that ties directly into the numbers Salt Security published today: forty-nine point eight percent of organizations have confirmed that an AI agent has already performed an unintended or unauthorized action over the past year. Nearly one in two. And yet only twelve point five percent have full visibility into what their agents are actually doing, from the initial prompt all the way through to MCP servers and the APIs being called.

Maya: Those numbers are genuinely striking. Almost half of organizations impacted, and barely one in eight actually knows what is going on under the hood.

Ryan: That is exactly why Salt Security is announcing new detection and response capabilities specifically built for AI agents. Their platform now maps suspicious LLM activity across all connected tools and APIs. It is a direct answer to that visibility gap.

Maya: And there is also something in the news today involving OpenAI and cybersecurity, right?

Ryan: Yes, and this one is very fresh. According to Gizmodo, OpenAI is reportedly preparing an imminent announcement of a model specialized in security, which would be called GPT-6 Cyber. It is reportedly designed to counter autonomous cyberattacks. The announcement could come as soon as the next few days, potentially timed around OpenAI Dev Day, scheduled for September 29th.

Maya: Is there any particular context around this announcement?

Ryan: Very particular context, yes. This is coming out after Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI, joined Dario Amodei, the CEO of Anthropic, in publicly calling for a slowdown in AI development. And the reason they cited is precisely the autonomous cyberattacks being generated by frontier models, meaning the most advanced AI systems. It is a significant signal when the leaders of the two biggest AI labs are themselves calling for caution.

Maya: It is almost paradoxical, right? You announce a security model while simultaneously asking for a slowdown.

Ryan: That is the permanent tension in this industry. You build tools to counter risks that you yourself had a hand in creating. We come back to this dynamic a lot on this show, and it is a debate that is nowhere close to being settled.

Maya: Let's move into the industry impact segment, and there are some major announcements here too. Let's start with finance.

Ryan: Big news from yesterday, September 24th: BNP Paribas and Google Cloud made it official, announcing a five-year partnership to deploy AI agents inside the bank's Corporate and Institutional Banking division. BNP Paribas will have access to Google Cloud's AI-optimized infrastructure, the Gemini models, and Gemini Enterprise.

Maya: Is this the first collaboration of its kind between a major bank and Google specifically around agentic AI?

Ryan: It is the first major documented collaboration between a top-tier bank and Google specifically focused on agentic AI in production. And it really marks a turning point, because banks are historically among the most conservative institutions when it comes to technology decisions. When a bank like BNP Paribas commits to a five-year deal, that sends a very loud signal to the rest of the financial sector.

Maya: Do we know what kinds of agents are actually going to be deployed?

Ryan: The scope announced covers the Corporate and Institutional Banking division, which handles large corporate clients and financial markets, among other things. The specific use cases are not yet public, but this is clearly a production deployment, not a pilot program.

Maya: And there is also movement in the pharmaceutical space.

Ryan: Yes, Insilico Medicine published their quarterly updates for Pharma.AI 2026. They are announcing specialized AI agents that now orchestrate entire pharmaceutical discovery workflows: ADMET prediction, which covers how a drug is absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and eliminated by the body, and the design of biologics, which are drugs derived from living organisms, in under seventy-two hours using generative models.

Maya: Seventy-two hours to design a biologic is genuinely revolutionary when you think about the typical timelines.

Ryan: Normal timelines run to months, sometimes years, for certain stages of this process. We are talking about a dramatic acceleration. And when you put that alongside Claude's discovery of that CRISPR-like enzymatic system, it illustrates a really powerful convergence between AI and the life sciences that is materializing in very concrete ways right now.

Maya: To round out the industry overview, there is also a report from IAB Europe on advertising.

Ryan: This one is a more nuanced note to end on. IAB Europe is out with their 2026 report on AI in digital advertising. The takeaway is clear: agentic ad buying is moving forward, organizations are working on it, but actual production deployment remains very limited. Professionals in the space are calling for shared frameworks and practical governance. In short, the ambition is far outpacing the reality on the ground.

Maya: That is really a mirror of what we are seeing across a lot of industries.

Ryan: Exactly. The gap between the hype and actual deployment is a recurring theme in 2026. Companies want to move, but operational complexity, governance questions, and security challenges are slowing down the path to scale.

Maya: Which brings us naturally to our wrap-up. Ryan, if you had to pull out three takeaways from today, what would they be?

Ryan: First, agentic AI is entering fundamental science with Claude's discovery at Anthropic, and that is probably the most structurally significant event of the day when you think about the long-term picture. Second, AI agent security is becoming a critical and urgent issue: the Salt Security numbers and the IBM vulnerability make that completely unambiguous. And third, major institutions, banks, pharmaceutical companies, are crossing the line from experimentation into real production deployment. We are not in the pilot phase anymore. This is happening in the real world.

Maya: Ryan, thank you so much for walking us through all of that. And thank you to everyone listening. AI Squared is your daily companion for keeping up with artificial intelligence without getting lost in the weeds. We will see you tomorrow for another episode. Talk to you then!
