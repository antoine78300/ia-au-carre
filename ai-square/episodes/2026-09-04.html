Full transcript

Maya: Artificial intelligence may have just crossed a historic threshold this Friday. OpenAI drops GPT-6 Astra and invokes the age of AGI, while the entire industry pivots hard into agentic mode. Stay with us, you're listening to AI Square.

Maya: Ryan, let's kick things off with the biggest earthquake of the day. OpenAI just deployed GPT-6 Astra, and the word AGI actually came up. Is this the real deal?

Ryan: This is probably the most symbolically loaded announcement we've seen in years, Maya. OpenAI is presenting GPT-6 Astra as crossing the threshold of artificial general intelligence. The model is setting new records in math and science, which are areas where benchmarks are notoriously demanding. And the rollout is starting with enterprise customers in cybersecurity, and that is no coincidence.

Maya: Why cybersecurity first?

Ryan: Because the stakes are enormous there, and you can measure a model's performance very quickly. If GPT-6 Astra can identify vulnerabilities or analyze threats at a level that surpasses human experts, you can actually prove that pretty fast. It's a strategic showcase. From there, the rollout gradually expands to Plus, Pro, and Business users, and through the API.

Maya: And this word AGI, how do we take that? OpenAI doesn't usually throw that term around lightly.

Ryan: Historically, OpenAI defined AGI as a system capable of outperforming humans at most economically valuable tasks. They seem to be saying now that GPT-6 Astra meets or exceeds that bar. That said, the scientific community is still deeply divided on what AGI even means, so some healthy skepticism is warranted. But what is not up for debate is that a model this powerful is going to force the entire industry to reposition itself very quickly.

Maya: And speaking of which, the other major players were definitely not sitting on their hands today either.

Ryan: Not at all. Anthropic unveiled Fable and Mythos, and Meta and Google both dropped new models as well. What is really striking about today is that every single one of these major players put agentic capabilities front and center. This is no longer a trend, it is a full industry-wide convergence around a single paradigm: AI systems that act autonomously, that plan, that use tools, that orchestrate complex tasks without constant human oversight.

Maya: And that is exactly what we are going to dig into in our second block, because today was absolutely packed on the agentic frameworks front. Several major infrastructure announcements came out. Where do we start?

Ryan: Let's start with Broadcom and VMware Tanzu, because this one is going to touch a huge number of enterprises. Broadcom is rolling out major architectural updates to VMware Tanzu Platform, positioning it as what they're calling an operational runtime for autonomous agents. Specifically, with VCF 9.1, companies already running on the VMware ecosystem can now deploy AI agents inside isolated execution environments using what they call declarative Agent Buildpacks.

Maya: Agent Buildpacks, what exactly is that? Can you break it down for us?

Ryan: Sure. Imagine you want to deploy an AI agent inside your company. Right now that is often a complex process that requires a lot of manual configuration. Agent Buildpacks are about standardizing and automating all of that. You declare what you want your agent to do, and the platform handles the rest. Think of it like what Docker containers did for application deployment about ten years ago. Broadcom is trying to do the same thing for AI agents.

Maya: And in that same spirit, CIQ released Fuzzball 4.2?

Ryan: Yes, and Fuzzball is interesting because it targets a very specific use case, which is high-performance computing, or HPC. CIQ designed Fuzzball 4.2 so that AI agents can directly operate the orchestration platform itself. Meaning the agent is not just analyzing data, it can actually spin up training or inference workloads in production on its own. And this version adds AMD GPU support, which significantly expands the hardware options available.

Maya: AMD keeps coming up in multiple announcements today, actually. We also have PACE integrating LangGraph.

Ryan: Exactly. AMD PACE, which is their orchestration platform built on EPYC processors, now integrates LangGraph. LangGraph is a tool that developers widely use to build multi-step agentic workflows. Basically, you can now describe complex workflows where multiple agents collaborate, each with different roles, handling reasoning, planning, and tool use, and all of it runs natively on AMD hardware. This is AMD's play to establish itself as a credible alternative to NVIDIA in the agentic ecosystem.

Maya: And to close out this frameworks block, GK Software is jumping into the game with a very sector-specific angle.

Ryan: Right, GK Software is targeting retail. They are launching GK Agentic, a specialized framework for large retailer operations. This is a really telling signal, because it shows that agentic frameworks are no longer confined to research labs or hyperscalers. They are now landing in very concrete industries, with specific use cases like inventory optimization, pricing management, and supply chain planning. Agentic AI is becoming a vertical product.

Maya: Now let's move to the research side, and there is a paper out of the National University of Singapore that is genuinely worth pausing on. Walk us through JIT-Agent.

Ryan: JIT-Agent is a genuinely novel approach. The problem it is trying to solve is the rigidity of today's AI agents. When you build an agent right now, you define its structure, its tools, its behavior upfront. That is called a harness. If the environment changes, you often have to rebuild the whole thing. The team in Singapore is proposing to turn that construction process itself into a learning capability. JIT stands for Just In Time.

Maya: So the agent learns to build itself?

Ryan: That is pretty close to it. JIT-Agent enables the synthesis of adaptive harnesses in real time, for any agentic language model. The agent observes its environment, understands what is being asked of it, and dynamically generates the structure it needs to complete the task. That is a real departure from the static approach that dominates today.

Maya: And the practical implications are significant?

Ryan: Very significant. Right now, deploying agents in fast-changing environments requires enormous ongoing maintenance. If JIT-Agent delivers on its promise, you could have agents that automatically adapt to new contexts without human intervention. For companies that want to use agents in dynamic processes like crisis management, customer support in unprecedented situations, or exploratory research, that is potentially transformational.

Maya: And on the applied research side, NVIDIA is not sitting this one out either.

Ryan: NVIDIA announced DeepSeek Harness today, a tool for local agentic coding workflows. The idea is to let agents code autonomously directly on your own machine, no cloud dependency required. And in parallel, they updated MLPerf Client to version 2.0, adding benchmarks specifically designed for agentic AI and image generation. This matters because the industry needed standardized measurement tools to compare agentic system performance. MLPerf is stepping up to be that reference.

Maya: Alright, we're moving into our final block on industry impact, and this is really where you start to see how all of this is playing out in the day-to-day reality of businesses. Let's start with finance, because there are two connected stories there.

Ryan: Yes, and they really complement each other. On one side, you have Kyndryl teaming up with Incore Bank and Google Cloud to automate the KYC process, which is the customer identity verification required when opening a bank account. Thanks to Gemini agents, the onboarding process becomes largely autonomous. The agent collects information, verifies documents, cross-references data, and either validates the application or escalates to a human when needed.

Maya: That is a massive time-saver for banks.

Ryan: Enormous. KYC is currently one of the most expensive and time-consuming processes in banking. Cutting that from several days down to a matter of minutes is a huge competitive advantage. And the other finance story of the day is Anthropic positioning Claude inside the agentic commerce ecosystem. EMVCo, which is the organization behind card payment standards, is working on a framework for secure agentic payments, and Anthropic is integrating Claude into that ecosystem.

Maya: Agentic payments is a phrase we're hearing more and more. What does that actually look like in practice?

Ryan: Picture an AI agent managing your corporate purchasing autonomously. It receives a request, identifies the supplier, negotiates the price, and triggers the payment, all on its own. For that to work at scale and securely, you need identity and authentication standards built specifically for agents. That is what EMVCo and Anthropic are building together. September 30th has been mentioned as a target for initial feedback on this framework, so we are very much in an active standardization phase right now.

Maya: And to wrap things up, there is WeatherNext 3 from Google DeepMind, which is a great reminder that agentic AI is not just a business story.

Ryan: WeatherNext 3 is fascinating. Google DeepMind is presenting this model as the most accurate global weather forecasting model ever built. What sets it apart is that it learns directly from real-time observations and generates high-resolution hourly forecasts. This is not traditional statistical weather modeling, it is a model that continuously adapts to incoming data. Applications range from agriculture to disaster response, aviation, and energy management. And it is a great example of a case where AI clearly surpasses human capabilities in a very concrete and consequential domain.

Maya: Ryan, if we step back and look at the full picture of today, the word that keeps coming up everywhere is agentic. Is this really the defining turn of 2026?

Ryan: It is the turn that is happening right now, yes. What we are seeing today is convergence happening at every level simultaneously. Foundation models like GPT-6 Astra are now powerful enough to reason and plan autonomously. Frameworks like Tanzu, Fuzzball, and PACE make it possible to deploy those agents reliably in production. Standards like the ones EMVCo is developing are starting to secure interactions between agents and financial systems. And real-world use cases, from banking to retail to weather forecasting, are multiplying fast. We are not in the experimental phase anymore.

Maya: That is a wrap on today's edition of AI Square. A day that will probably go down in the books, with GPT-6 Astra and this question of AGI making the leap from theoretical debate to actual product announcement. There is still a lot to unpack, and we will do exactly that tomorrow, with fresh news, new developments, and the first reactions from the scientific community to everything that went down today. Thanks so much for listening, and we will see you tomorrow on AI Square.
