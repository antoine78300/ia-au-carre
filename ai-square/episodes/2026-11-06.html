Maya: Welcome to AI Squared, your daily dose of everything you need to know about artificial intelligence. Maya: Ryan, let's kick things off with a big story on the regulatory side. The OCC, the federal banking regulator, just dropped a formalized supervision framework called Bulletin 2026-54. What does this actually mean for banks on the ground?

Ryan: This is a really significant move, Maya. The OCC, which oversees national banks across the US, officially put this framework on paper on November 4th, 2026. It's specifically designed to evaluate how banks are using agentic AI systems in their day-to-day operations, and we're talking about areas like credit decisions and fraud detection, two spaces where AI is already deeply embedded.

Maya: So what makes this different from what regulators were doing before?

Ryan: What's really interesting here is the philosophy behind it. The OCC isn't treating AI as some separate, isolated risk category off in its own silo. Instead, the framework is built on the idea that AI introduces, amplifies, or modifies risks that already existed. And what drives the regulatory examination isn't how technically sophisticated the model is or how complex the architecture looks, it's the real-world impact on banking operations.

Maya: In other words, it doesn't matter how impressive your algorithm is on paper. What matters is what it's actually doing out in the world.

Ryan: Exactly. And there's another key piece here: this framework fills a gap that was left wide open by the April 2026 guidance, Bulletin 2026-13, which explicitly excluded generative and agentic AI from its scope. The 2026-54 closes that loophole. And to make sure no one plays the waiting game, the OCC is clear that this new framework applies right now, even if other regulators are still working on joint model risk management guidance that incorporates generative AI.
